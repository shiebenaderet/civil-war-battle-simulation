# v3.12 "Acts of the War" — Design Spec

**Date:** 2026-05-05
**Author:** Shie Benaderet (with Claude as collaborator)
**Roadmap position:** v3.12, second of five versions toward v4.0 launch
**Status:** approved for implementation planning
**Predecessor:** v3.11.0 (shipped 2026-05-05) — Documentary Pass / Field Report aesthetic
**Parent spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-05-04-launch-readiness-design.md`

---

## 1. Problem Statement

After v3.11, the simulation looks like a documentary but is still structured as 13 isolated battles. Students leave knowing 13 battles happened in some order — but cannot describe the war as a story with a shape, cannot tell you why 1863 was the turning point, and cannot connect Vicksburg + Gettysburg in the same week to "this is when the Confederacy started losing."

Per the parent spec, v3.12 is the highest-leverage version in the roadmap. It addresses the most important gap: the war as a story.

## 2. Diagnosis (re-stated, briefly)

The four-act structure already exists in code. `js/ui.js:279` defines `groupedReflections` — four entries with themes, battle ranges, prompts at three reading levels, teacher tips, and sentence starters. The `reflectionBattles = [2, 5, 8, 12]` array marks the act endings. The content is uniformly excellent.

What's missing is **surfacing**. Students implicitly experience the act structure (every 3-4 battles → grouped reflection) but they don't perceive it as a structure. The acts have no name, no location-in-time, no "here's what's about to happen" framing, and no retrieval check before students are asked to reflect on themes across battles they may have only skimmed.

v3.12 surfaces the existing structure with two new components:

- **Act intros** — a ~10-12 second cinematic interstitial before battles 0, 3, 6, and 9
- **Act retrieval moments** — 3 quick-recall multiple-choice questions before each grouped reflection (battles 2, 5, 8, 12)

Both use the existing four themes from `groupedReflections` as their content backbone.

## 3. Goals (and what we will NOT do)

### Goals
- Students perceive the war as four acts, each with a distinct character.
- Before any grouped reflection, students have just demonstrated retrieval of three concrete facts from that act.
- Students cannot click through the new components without thinking.
- Historical accuracy is non-negotiable. Every new sentence and every retrieval question's correct answer must trace to an existing line of content in `js/data/battles.js`.

### Non-goals (deliberately out of scope)
- No new battle content. Existing 13 battles remain exactly as-is.
- No changes to free-play mode. Acts apply only to historical mode.
- No analytics or teacher dashboard for retrieval performance.
- No changes to the reflection textarea (Guardrail 3 deferred to v3.14, where the reflection screen is being restructured anyway).
- No changes to PDF export.
- No new game modes.
- No structural change to the battle screen itself (that's v3.14's job).

| Act | Battles (idx) | Battles (named) | Theme | Reflection battle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | 0–2 | Fort Sumter, Bull Run, Shiloh | The War Begins | Shiloh (idx 2) |
| II | 3–5 | Antietam, Fredericksburg, Chancellorsville | The Human Cost | Chancellorsville (idx 5) |

## 5. The Act Intro Component

### 5.1 Visual treatment (decided: "animated dispatch", with fallback)

The act intro is a full-screen interstitial that fires before battles at index 0, 3, 6, and 9. The treatment has two possible forms:

**Form B (target): Animated dispatch.** A short choreographed sequence:
1. Dateline appears (act number, year range)
2. SVG outline of the relevant theater draws itself via `stroke-dasharray` animation (~1.5s)
3. 3–4 markers fade in sequentially with location names (~1s each)
4. Headline (act name) fades in
5. One positioning sentence fades in
6. Continue button enabled

Total runtime: ~10-12 seconds.

**Form C (fallback): Period letter.** Reuses the existing `.letter-paper` chrome. A short dispatch from a recurring narrator voice, presented as a dated period document. No animation required beyond a fade-in.

### 5.2 Two-phase rollout (Act I prototype, then fork)

To manage risk on the highest-effort visual decision in v3.12:

**Phase 1 (~3 days):**
- Author all four acts' content into `js/data/acts.js`.
- Build Act I (Form B) end-to-end: SVG eastern-theater map, marker animation, gate logic.
- Build the plumbing all acts will share (advanceHistorical changes, new screen states, save/resume).
- Acts II–IV remain unwired. Students would experience them via the existing battle→reflection flow during Phase 1.

**Phase 1 review point.** Real visual review of Act I in browser.

**Phase 2 (~2 days, conditional):**
- **If Act I lands:** build SVG maps for Acts II, III, IV and wire them up.
- **If Act I doesn't land:** fall back to Form C for all four acts using the existing letter-paper chrome. Retrieval moment design is identical in both branches; only the intro visual differs.

The fork happens after a real visual review of Act I — no upfront commitment to building four maps if the first one doesn't justify the work.

### 5.3 Content per act intro

Each act intro renders:

- **Act number** ("I" / "II" / "III" / "IV") — typewriter font, small uppercase
- **Date range** ("1861–1862" / "1862–1863" / etc.) — typewriter font
- **Map** — SVG outline + markers; 3-4 markers per act corresponding to the battles in that act
- **Headline** — act name from the canonical table above, set in serif uppercase ink
- **Positioning sentence** — one sentence, three reading levels (drafted under "content authoring" below)

### 5.4 Skippability and read-time gate (Guardrail 1)

The Continue button is **hidden until the animation sequence completes** (~10-12 seconds). Once visible, it pulses subtly. The act intro is fully skippable after that, but cannot be blasted through during the animation. Students with `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` get the final state immediately AND a 6-second minimum read time before Continue appears, so they're not penalized but also not given a free pass.

### 5.5 First-time vs. resume

Each act intro fires **once per playthrough**. State is tracked in `gameState.shownActIntros = [0, 1, 2, ...]`. A student who saves mid-Act-II and resumes will not re-watch Act I or II intros; they'll see Act III's intro when they reach battle 6. A student who clicks "Start Over" gets a fresh state and re-experiences all intros.

## 6. The Act Retrieval Component

### 6.1 When it fires

The retrieval moment fires **after** the "What Happened" step of battles 2, 5, 8, 12 (the existing reflection battles), and **before** the existing grouped reflection screen.

Flow on a reflection battle:
```
Briefing → Your Call → What Happened
  → [NEW: Act Retrieval]
  → Grouped Reflection
  → Continue to next battle
```

### 6.2 Format (decided: multiple choice, must-get-right gate)

Three multiple-choice questions per act, four options each.

For each question:
- Student selects an option.
- **First wrong attempt:** display "Not quite — try again." with a brief, vague nudge that points toward the right area of thinking but does not give away the answer.
- **Second wrong attempt:** reveal the correct answer with the explanation, framed as "The answer is X because…" Student is asked to read carefully before proceeding.
- **Correct attempt (any time):** show the explanation as positive reinforcement ("Right — [explanation]"). The explanation reading is part of the learning even when the student got the answer right immediately.
- Continue button to the next question is **disabled until the correct option has been selected** — even if it took two tries with the answer revealed.

After all three questions are answered correctly, Continue advances to the existing grouped reflection screen.

### 6.3 Question authoring constraints

Every retrieval question must satisfy three conditions:

1. **Source-grounded.** The correct answer must trace to a specific section of a specific battle in `js/data/battles.js`. The `source` field in `js/data/acts.js` records this trace ("battles[2].historical.whatHappened.intermediate" or similar). Distractor answers should be plausible but identifiably wrong if the student remembers the content.
2. **Within the reading level.** Beginner questions ask about concrete facts ("Who won at Bull Run?"). Intermediate questions ask about cause and effect ("Why did the Confederates win at Bull Run?"). Advanced questions ask about analysis ("What did Bull Run reveal about both sides' assumptions?").
3. **Three reading levels.** The student's chosen difficulty (beginner / intermediate / advanced) determines which set of three questions they see. The questions are not the same questions translated; they're calibrated to each level.

That means we author **4 acts × 3 questions × 3 reading levels = 36 retrieval questions total**, plus their explanations and citations.

### 6.4 Visual treatment

The retrieval moment uses the existing Field Report card chrome from v3.11:
- Each question on its own card
- Multiple-choice options as Field Report buttons (sharp-cornered, ink-on-paper, with letter prefix A/B/C/D via `data-letter` like the WWYD options)
- Correct selection: option highlights with `--color-success`
- Incorrect selection: option highlights with `--color-error`, "Try again" message appears below
- Explanation appears as a small bordered footnote beneath the question after the correct answer is selected

Mobile-first: questions stack vertically, options stack vertically, all tap targets ≥44px.

## 9. SVG Maps

Four SVG maps total, one per act, hand-authored. Stored inline in `js/data/acts.js` as template literals (consistent with how the `maps` data file is structured today) so no external file fetch is required (offline classroom constraint preserved).

Each SVG has:
- A theater outline (eastern theater for Acts I/II; eastern + western combined for III/IV — the war's geography demanded both by 1863)
- Optional state borders rendered as faint lines
- Path elements with class `.act-marker-anchor` at each battle's normalized coords
- `viewBox` set so the map scales gracefully on mobile

The outline path uses `stroke-dasharray` with `pathLength: 1` so the draw-in animation is consistent regardless of path length.

For Phase 1 (Act I prototype), only the Act I SVG is authored. If Phase 2 commits to Form B, the other three are authored then.

## 10. Failure Modes and Error Handling

| Failure | Handling |
|---|---|
| JS error during act intro animation | Falls through to static (non-animated) version: headline, sentence, no map. Continue is enabled. Student is not trapped. |
| SVG fails to load (offline edge case, malformed path) | Skip the map markers; show headline + positioning sentence only. |
| `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` | All animations skip to final state. Continue gate becomes a 6-second minimum read time instead of an animation-completion gate. |
| Retrieval state corruption (saved game from a bad version, malformed `completedRetrievals`) | Treat all retrievals as not-yet-completed. Worst case: a student replays a retrieval. Acceptable. |
| Student gets all three retrieval questions right on first try | Show explanations as positive reinforcement. Reading the explanation is part of the learning even when correct. |
| Student gets a question wrong twice and the answer is auto-revealed | Continue still requires they click the (now-highlighted) correct answer. They can't skip without acknowledging it. |
| `acts.js` data is missing for an act (developer error) | Log warning, fall through to existing flow without showing the intro/retrieval for that act. Don't crash the game. |

## 11. Historical Accuracy Guardrails

These are non-negotiable acceptance criteria for any new content shipping in v3.12:

1. **Every fact has a citation.** Every retrieval question's correct answer points to a specific section of a specific battle in `js/data/battles.js` via the `source` field. Every act intro's positioning sentence cites its source content the same way.
2. **Citations are verified.** Before merging Phase 1, a one-time validation script reads `js/data/acts.js` and confirms each `source` string resolves to a non-empty value in `battles.js`. Orphaned citations block the merge.
3. **No causal oversimplification.** Specifically prohibited: "Lincoln freed the slaves with the Emancipation Proclamation" (the proclamation only applied to Confederate-held territory; full emancipation came with the 13th Amendment). Each act intro and explanation must distinguish "what happened" from "what was sufficient." Author-time review checks for this pattern explicitly.
4. **Numbers and dates spot-checked.** Every casualty figure, every date, every named figure is checked against the corresponding battle in `battles.js`. Discrepancies are reconciled (usually by trusting the existing `battles.js`, since v3.10 already curated those carefully).
5. **Side-symmetric framing.** Where a question or explanation could be told from either side's perspective, the framing should not embed a side. Example bad: "Why did our victory at Antietam matter?" Example good: "Why did the Union victory at Antietam allow Lincoln to issue the Emancipation Proclamation?"

## 12. Content Authoring Approach (decided: option C from brainstorming)

I draft all content with citations in place; user reviews before any of it is committed. Process:

1. Author `js/data/acts.js` populated with all positioning sentences and retrieval questions, including `source` citations on every entry, in three reading levels.
2. User reviews the file, with my self-review notes alongside (any phrases I was uncertain about, any places where I had to make a judgment call about emphasis).
3. After approval, the implementation plan integrates the file as part of Phase 1.

## 13. Acceptance Criteria

For Phase 1 to be considered done:
- All four acts have complete content in `js/data/acts.js` (12 positioning sentences, 36 retrieval questions, all with citations).
- A validation pass confirms every citation resolves.
- Act I intro renders end-to-end with the map animation.
- Act I retrieval moment fires correctly after the "What Happened" step of Shiloh and before the existing grouped reflection.
- Save/resume works: a student saved mid-Act-II resumes without re-watching Act I.
- The Continue gate on the act intro behaves as designed (animation-complete on full-motion, 6-second timer on reduced-motion).
- Acts II–IV are unwired but their content is in `acts.js`.

For Phase 2 (Form B branch):
- Acts II, III, IV each have an SVG map authored.
- Each act's intro renders end-to-end.

For Phase 2 (Form C fallback branch):
- All four acts render their intro using the existing `.letter-paper` chrome.

## 14. Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| The animated map for Act I doesn't look good enough to justify Form B | Medium | Phase 1 review point. Falling back to Form C is cheap. |
| Retrieval questions feel too easy (kids click through without thinking) | Medium | Guardrail 2 (must-get-right) + the second-attempt nudge pattern. Re-examine after first classroom trial. |
| Authoring 36 retrieval questions with citations takes longer than estimated | Medium-high | If Phase 1 content authoring slips by >1 day, defer Acts II–IV retrieval to a v3.12.x patch. Act I retrieval ships with Phase 1 regardless. |
| A retrieval question's correct answer turns out not to be supported by the source content | High likelihood at least once across 36 questions | The validation script catches structural orphans; a manual content review pass before commit catches semantic ones. |
| Save/resume has an edge case that re-fires an act intro mid-act | Low | Test the save→resume path explicitly during Phase 1 QA. |

## 15. Out of Scope (final, named explicitly)

- Animations beyond CSS transitions and `setTimeout` choreography (no animation libraries).
- Per-question analytics or telemetry.
- Teacher dashboard for retrieval performance.
- Reflection textarea changes (Guardrail 3, deferred to v3.14).
- New battle content.
- Free-play mode changes.
- PDF export changes (could add a small "retrieval summary" later as a polish item).
- Localization beyond English.
