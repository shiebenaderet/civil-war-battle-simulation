# Battle Journal redesign — one coherent flow

**Date:** 2026-05-30. Replaces the disconnected "page 1 log / page 2 essay" split with a single arc where every capture feeds the driving question. Applies to all four tier handouts.

## Driving question (the spine)

**How was the Union able to defeat the Confederacy?**

Printed on the journal header and repeated at each synthesis point. Every capture is framed as collecting evidence for it.

## The three-part arc

```
Part 1: Battle Log (13 battles)  ->  Part 2: Act Checkpoints (4)  ->  Part 3: Final Answer
  per-battle key idea                  one evidence line per act        thesis from the 4 lines
```

The student should always see the chain: today's "why it mattered" note → the act's "one way this helped the Union win" line → the final essay's three reasons. The journal assembles itself into the essay.

---

## Part 1 — Battle Log

Four act sections (Act I-IV headings with years). Under each, one row per battle (13 total, 3/3/3/4). Each row:
- **Battle name**
- **My choice** (1 write-line) + **Matched history? ☐ Yes ☐ No**
- **Why it mattered** — "sum up the key idea in your own words" (2 write-lines; students summarize, do NOT copy the on-screen key idea)

Battles by act:
- Act I · The War Begins · 1861-1862: Fort Sumter, Bull Run, Shiloh
- Act II · A New Kind of War · 1862-1863: Antietam, Fredericksburg, Chancellorsville
- Act III · The Tide Turns · 1863: Vicksburg, Gettysburg, Chickamauga
- Act IV · The War's Legacy · 1864-1865: Wilderness, Atlanta, Sherman's March, Appomattox

## Part 2 — Act Checkpoint (after each act's battle rows; 4 total)

Each checkpoint box has TWO things:
1. **Act reflection prompt** — the tier-matched thinking question (existing text; see tier-text reference). 3 write-lines.
2. **NEW bridge line — "One way this act helped the Union win:"** a single write-line. This is the evidence the student banks toward the final question. Four of these become the raw material for the final essay's three reasons.

Label the bridge line clearly as evidence, e.g. a small tag "EVIDENCE FOR THE BIG QUESTION".

## Part 3 — Final Answer (was "page 2")

Reframed to pull directly from Part 2, not start cold. Opening framing line:
> "Look back at your four 'One way this act helped the Union win' lines. You already have your evidence. Now build your answer."

Then (keep, but connect):
- **The driving question** restated: "How was the Union able to defeat the Confederacy? Use at least three specific advantages or events."
- **Thesis frame** (Q1): "The Union defeated the Confederacy because ___, ___, and ___." (pull 3 of the 4 act lines)
- **Evidence rows** (Q2): 3 reasons, each with a battle/person/event that proves it (drawn from the battle log)
- **Word bank** (support): the 8 vocab terms, "circle each when you find it in the game"
- **Q3:** which word-bank term was most important to the Union's victory, and why
- **Q4:** the South had brave soldiers and skilled generals too; why were bravery and leadership not enough?

## Tier scaling

Same content arc across all 4 tiers; scaffolding scales:
- **★ Most Support (extra):** stacked single-column, largest font, sentence starters under every write-line ("This battle mattered because...", "This act helped the Union win because..."), word bank with definitions, simplest Q-set. Uses BEGINNER reflection text (no `extra` reflection exists; documented fallback).
- **★★ More Support (beginner):** sentence starters on the why-it-mattered and bridge lines; full word bank; BEGINNER reflection text.
- **★★★ Standard (intermediate):** the approved baseline; light scaffolding; INTERMEDIATE reflection text. (Already built; needs Part 2 bridge line + Part 3 reframing added.)
- **★★★★ Extra Challenge (advanced):** least scaffolding, no sentence starters, trimmed word bank or terms-only (no definitions), ADVANCED reflection text. Most open writing space.
